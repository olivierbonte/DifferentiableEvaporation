Model

EvaporationModel.ProcessBasedModel — Type
ProcessBasedModel{FT}(; forcings, parameters, t_span, u0, saveat, kwargs...)

Process-based model of the soil and canopy water balance, solved with solve! after initialize!.

The ODE state is u0 = [w_1, w_2, w_r] plus one accumulator per diagnostic in accumulated (default (:λE_tot, :H_tot, :G)): its time integral, a pure quadrature variable as in CVODES, left out of the error control (see state_tolerances).

The states are saved at $t_k =$ saveat[k], $k = 1, …, n + 1$, and the accumulated diagnostics are averaged over the windows $[t_k, t_{k+1}]$, $k = 1, …, n$: no window starts at the last time $t_{n+1}$ (e.g. one forcing time step after the last forcing time stamp).

solver_kwargs (default abstol = reltol = 1e-6, as SUMMA-SUNDIALS, Spiteri et al., 2024) are stored in model.prob and used by solve!; keyword arguments of solve! take precedence.

The forcing is constant within each window between the tstops (by default saveat). Each forcing in forcings is a vector with one value per window or a number for a constant forcing (see forcing_table); lon and utc_offset are numbers. Every evaluation within a window, also at its end, uses that window's forcing (see ModelParameters and window_callback). Change the parameters of model.prob with with_parameters. model.f_diagnostics(u, θ, t) gives the diagnostics at any t.

EvaporationModel.solve! — Method
solve!(model::ProcessBasedModel; yaxarray_output=false, clamp_states=false, kwargs...)

Solve the model, saving the states at model.saveat, and store the window_means of the accumulated diagnostics in model.diagnostics. kwargs (e.g. alg, callback, tstops) are passed to OrdinaryDiffEq.solve; extra tstops come on top of the window bounds. yaxarray_output=true stores the states and window means in a YAXArrays Dataset in model.output; off by default as it interferes with AD.

Implicit solvers take their Jacobian from ForwardDiff, ImplicitEuler(; autodiff=AutoForwardDiff()), or Enzyme, ImplicitEuler(; autodiff=AutoEnzyme(; function_annotation=EvaporationModel.Enzyme.Duplicated)) (Duplicated because the right-hand side is a closure).

clamp_states=true clamps the states to their physical range after every step with state_clamping_callback (applied after a user callback). Meant to be combined with fixed-step explicit Euler: solve!(model; alg=Euler(), dt, adaptive=false, clamp_states=true).

EvaporationModel.state_clamping_callback — Method
state_clamping_callback(model::ProcessBasedModel)

Callback clamping the physical states to their range after every step, $0 ≤ w_1, w_2 ≤ w_{sat}$ and $0 ≤ w_r ≤ w_{r,max}$ (max_canopy_capacity of the LAI of the current window), as in classic fixed-step hydrological models. Meant for explicit Euler at a fixed time step, see solve! with clamp_states=true.

Hard clamps: not mass conserving (the clipped water is not accounted for in the accumulated diagnostics) and with a zero derivative with respect to the state while a bound is active.

source
EvaporationModel.state_tolerances — Method
state_tolerances(model::ProcessBasedModel, solver_kwargs::NamedTuple)

solver_kwargs with the scalar abstol and reltol for the physical states replaced by one tolerance per state. Only scalar tolerances are allowed.

OrdinaryDiffEq scales the error of each state $i$ as $e_i / (\mathrm{abstol}_i + \max(|u_{\mathrm{prev},i}|, |u_i|)\,\mathrm{reltol}_i)$ and takes the root mean square over all $n$ states (Timestepping Method Descriptions). The same norm is used in the Newton convergence test of implicit solvers. The accumulators get abstol = Inf, so their scaled error is 0 and they are left out of the error control, as CVODES does by default for pure quadratures. The physical-state tolerances are scaled by $\sqrt{3/n}$, so that the norm equals the root mean square over the 3 physical states only: the time steps and physical states are then the same as without accumulators (for implicit solvers up to the Newton tolerance, as the Newton solve includes the accumulators). Per-state tolerances are passed to the solver as vectors, see Common Solver Options. Give both abstol and reltol: a missing one keeps its unscaled solver default.

EvaporationModel.ModelParameters — Type
ModelParameters(θ, forcing_table, window_starts, window=1)

The p of the model's ODEProblem: the model parameters θ (field tunable), the forcing value of every window (forcing_table) and the index of the current window, kept up to date by window_callback.

A SciMLStructures.jl structure, like InversionBinder in ODINN.jl. θ is the Tunable portion; set it with with_parameters. The field has to be called tunable (as in ModelingToolkit's MTKParameters): DiffEqBase's Enzyme rule for solve only adds the gradient to a field with that name, otherwise reverse-mode gradients are zero. The window index is the Discrete portion, so the adjoint solve restores it at every window change.

EvaporationModel.window_callback — Method
window_callback()

Keeps the window index of the ModelParameters up to date. It moves to the next window only after the step ending at a window bound is accepted, so every stage of that step still uses the old window's forcing. At the start of the solve it adds all window starts as tstops, so no step crosses a bound.

A window change counts as a derivative discontinuity (the default for a callback), so FSAL methods like Tsit5 recompute their first stage with the new forcing and SciMLSensitivity records the event. Ribasim does the same in update_basin!. Added to model.prob by initialize!.

EvaporationModel.fraction_wet_vegetation — Function
fraction_wet_vegetation(w_r, w_rmax, t=HardThresholds())

Fraction of the foliage covered with water $f_{wet} = (w_r / w_{rmax})^{2/3}$ [-], from Deardorff (1978), with the power taken by nonneg_pow (zero for $w_r < 0$) and $f_{wet}$ capped at one.

EvaporationModel.fraction_wet_vegetation_with_condensation — Method
fraction_wet_vegetation_with_condensation(f_wet, λE_pc)

Effective wet fraction of the canopy, $f_{wet,eff} = f_{wet} + (1 - f_{wet})(1 - g)$, with $g$ the Heaviside step of the potential canopy latent heat flux λE_pc [W m⁻²] (Penman-Monteith with zero surface resistance).

When the canopy condenses ($λE_{pc} < 0$, so $g = 0$), $f_{wet} = 1$: there is no transpiration and the dew goes into the interception store. Otherwise $f_{wet,eff} = f_{wet}$.

The step is hard for every threshold treatment. A smoothed step would leave $f_{wet,eff} > f_{wet}$ while the canopy evaporates ($λE_{pc} > 0$), an interception loss that is not bounded by the canopy store, which then drains below zero.

EvaporationModel.ground_heat_flux — Function
ground_heat_flux(::SantanelloFriedl03, R_ns, w_1, w_sat, t_sol, t=HardThresholds();
c_gmin=0.31, c_gmax=0.35, t_gmin=74_000, t_gmax=100_000, phase_shift=10_800, p_n=0.5)

Ground heat flux $G = r R_{ns}$ [W m⁻²], with R_ns the net radiation at the soil surface [W m⁻²] and t_sol the time since solar noon [s] - negative before noon. The ratio $r$ switches from a daytime to a nighttime value with the sign of R_ns, $r = k\, r_{day} + (1 - k)\, r_{night}$ with $k$ = step_function of R_ns under the threshold treatment t.

During the day ($R_{ns} > 0$), $r_{day} = c_g \cos(2π (t_{sol} + t_{shift}) / t_g)$, Eq. 4 of Santanello & Friedl (2003), who only fitted this equation for positive $G$. It is applied to R_ns as in ALEXI (Anderson et al. 2018, Eq. 15), with $c_g$ and $t_g$ interpolated linearly in $Θ = w_1 / w_{sat}$ as in Mallick et al. (2022), Eqs. S1.19-S1.20.

At night ($R_{ns} < 0$), the constant ratio r_{night}, as recommended by Liebethal & Foken (2007) to complement the daytime Santanello & Friedl function, with $p = 0.5$ Allen et al., 1998 Eq. 46

EvaporationModel.c_1 — Function
c_1(w_1, w_sat, b, c_1sat, w_wp, t=HardThresholds())

Compute force coefficient c_1 [-] of force restore framework for soil mositure, $C_1 = C_{1sat} (w_{sat} / \max(w_1, w_{wp}))^{b/2 + 1}$. See equation 20 of Noilhan & Mahfouf, 1996. w_1 is floored at the wilting point, the lower validity bound of the formula, with a max under the threshold treatment t.

Arguments

  • w_1: Surface soil moisutre [m³ m⁻³]
  • w_sat: Saturated soil moisture [m³ m⁻³]
  • b: the Brooks-Corey/Clapp-Hornberger parameter, see compute_b
  • c_1sat: $C_1$ at saturation for the chosen d_1, see c_1sat
  • w_wp: Soil moisture at wilting point [m³ m⁻³]
source
EvaporationModel.c_1sat — Method
c_1sat(::NoilhanMahfouf96, x_clay, d_1)
c_1sat(::NoilhanPlanton89, w_sat, b, ψ_sat, K_sat, d_1)

Force coefficient $C_{1sat}$ [-], the value of c_1 at $w_1 = w_{sat}$, for a surface layer of normalization depth d_1 [m].

$C_1$ is dimensionless and proportional to d_1: Eq. A4 of Noilhan & Planton (1989) gives $C_1 = 2 d_1 / d$, with $d$ the penetration depth of the diurnal soil moisture wave. Only $C_1 / d_1$ enters the force-restore equation, so any d_1 gives the same dynamics as long as $C_{1sat}$ is computed for that same d_1.

  • NoilhanMahfouf96: regression on the clay percentage x_clay [%], Eq. 32 of Noilhan & Mahfouf (1996). The regression is normalized to $d_1 = 1$ m (as used in the SURFEX and WRF Pleim–Xiu code), so it is scaled by d_1 / 1 m here.
  • NoilhanPlanton89: from the soil hydraulic properties, Eq. A5 of Noilhan & Planton (1989), $C_{1sat} = 2 \sqrt{\pi} d_1 \sqrt{w_{sat} / (b |ψ_{sat}| K_{sat} τ)}$, with w_sat [m³ m⁻³], the Clapp–Hornberger b [-], the saturated matric potential ψ_sat [m], the saturated hydraulic conductivity K_sat [m s⁻¹] and $τ$ = 1 day. Representative values per soil texture are given in Table 2 of Clapp & Hornberger (1978) (there $ψ_{sat}$ in cm and $K_{sat}$ in 10⁻⁴ cm s⁻¹; convert to m and m s⁻¹).

Examples

With the loam row of Table 2 of Clapp & Hornberger (1978) ($w_{sat}$ = 0.451, $b$ = 5.39, $ψ_{sat}$ = 47.8 cm, $K_{sat}$ = 6.95 × 10⁻⁴ cm s⁻¹) and $d_1 = 0.1$ m, Eq. A5 gives the Noilhan & Planton (1989) table value of 0.191, close to the clay regression at 19% clay:

using EvaporationModel
c_np = c_1sat(NoilhanPlanton89(), 0.451, 5.39, 0.478, 6.95e-6, 0.1)
c_nm = c_1sat(NoilhanMahfouf96(), 19.0, 0.1)
round(c_np; digits=3), round(c_nm; digits=3)

# output

(0.191, 0.191)

EvaporationModel.surface_infiltration_factor — Function
surface_infiltration_factor(w_1, w_sat, m_1=0.01)

Share of the infiltration I_s that wets the surface layer, $f_1 = 1 - e^{(w_1 - w_{sat})/m_1}$ [-],It keepsw_1 \le w_{sat}` without clipping the state:

This is the continuous counterpart of the clip of w_g at w_sat in SURFEX (hydro_soil.F90), which produces no runoff: w_1 is not a water store, it only represent as thin surface layer. w_2 still receives all of I_sand the water balance is unchanged. The kernel is the exponential smoothing kernel of Eq. 20 in Kavetski & Kuczera (2007) (see exponential_kernel). It is always smooth, whatever the threshold treatment: a model formulation, not a threshold. m_1 [m³ m⁻³] sets the width of the transition.

EvaporationModel.soil_evaporation_efficiency — Function
soil_evaporation_efficiency(approach::Pielke92, w_1, w_fc, t=HardThresholds())
soil_evaporation_efficiency(approach::Martens17, w_1, w_res, w_c, t=HardThresholds())

Calculate soil evaporation efficiency β, a factor between 0 and 1 which scales the potential soil evaporation

Arguments

  • approach: The approach to use for calculating soil evaporation efficiency, a subtype of SoilEvaporationEfficiencyMethod
  • w_1: Soil moisture from first layer [m³/m³]
  • t: ThresholdTreatment for the bounds on w_1 (Pielke92) or β (Martens17); default HardThresholds

With approach = Pielke92(), the following inputs are

  • w_fc: Soil moisture at field capacity [m³/m³]

With approach = Martens17(), the following inputs are

  • w_res: Residual soil moisture [m³/m³]
  • w_c: Critical soil moisture [m³/m³]

Details

With approach = Pielke92(), β is calculated using euqation (7) of Lee & Pielke (1992), with β = 1 for w_1 ≥ w_fc and β = 0 for w_1 ≤ 0: w_1 is clamped to $[0, w_{fc}]$ with a using clamp under t.

With approach = Martens17(), β is calculated using equation (6) of Martens et al. (2017): β is clamped to $[0, 1]$ under t.

Examples

using EvaporationModel
w_fc = 0.35
w_1 = w_fc / 2
β_p = soil_evaporation_efficiency(Pielke92(), w_1, w_fc)
β_fc = soil_evaporation_efficiency(Pielke92(), w_fc, w_fc)
β_wet = soil_evaporation_efficiency(Pielke92(), 1.5 * w_fc, w_fc)
β_p ≈ 0.25 && β_fc ≈ 1 && β_wet ≈ 1
